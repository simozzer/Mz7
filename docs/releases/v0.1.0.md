The first Mz7: a DX7 for your DAW.

## Downloads

- **Mz7-0.1.0-win64-Setup.exe** - Windows: the installer, for the VST3, the standalone app or
  both.
- **Mz7-0.1.0-win64-VST3.zip** and **Mz7-0.1.0-win64-Standalone.zip** - Windows: the same, to
  install by hand.
- **Mz7-0.1.0-macOS-beta.zip** - macOS (beta): the VST3, an Audio Unit and the standalone app,
  with an install script.
- **Mz7-0.1.0-linux-x86_64-beta.tar.gz** - Linux (beta): the VST3, an LV2 and the standalone
  app, with an install script.

**Windows:** Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. The installer is not code-signed, so Windows may warn
about an unknown publisher: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

**macOS:** Apple Silicon and Intel Macs, macOS 11 or later. Unzip, then in Terminal type
`bash ` (with a space), drag `install.sh` from the unzipped folder into the window, and press
Return.

**Linux:** unpack, then `bash install.sh` in the unpacked folder.

## What it is

- **A model of the DX7's sound chip:** six operators and the 32 algorithms, with true feedback
  loops in algorithms 4 and 6. It runs on the chip's log-sine and exponent tables, at its
  49,096 Hz, through its floating-point DAC.
- **The envelopes, LFO, keyboard scaling and velocity** as measured on real DX7s.
- **The DX7's own features:** sixteen voices, mono and portamento, and the mod wheel, foot
  pedal, breath controller and aftertouch.
- **128 original voices** in four cartridges - *Classic*, *Keys & Mallets*, *Bass & Lead*,
  *Pads & FX*. Any DX7 cartridge loads as a .syx, and saves as one.
- **Every parameter on one panel**, and every one can be automated.

Also in [MzDebirth](https://github.com/simozzer/MzDebirth) 0.13.0, as the rack's sixth
machine.
